## 2. Tagline Options

A tagline is not a slogan. It is the line that follows the logo on the homepage hero, in pitch decks, and on the email signature of every partner.

The five candidates below are ordered by recommendation strength.

### 1. *Engineered for the post-quantum decade.* — **Primary recommendation**

- Stakes the firm in the defining transition of the next ten years.
- "Engineered" signals delivery capability, not just advisory.
- Holds AI and blockchain as instruments inside the broader quantum-era posture.

### 2. *The post-quantum practice for Philippine institutions.*

- Direct, descriptive, claims category leadership.
- Best for use in formal contexts: tenders, regulator briefings, partnership decks.
- Less emotive; reads as factual.

### 3. *Cryptographic resilience. AI clarity. Verifiable trust.*

- The three-pillar architecture compressed into one line.
- Good for sub-tagline or pillar-section header rather than primary hero.

### 4. *Quantum-ready, by design.*

- Compact. Memorable. Implies foresight.
- Risk: "quantum-ready" is becoming a vendor cliché globally; works in PH market today, may not in 18 months.

### 5. *Strategy and systems for the institutions that come next.*

- Aspirational. Names the audience (institutions) and the temporal stance (what comes next).
- Slightly soft; better as a section header than a tagline.

### Style rules for any tagline we ship

- No exclamation marks.
- No verbs in the imperative ("Transform!" "Build!" "Scale!").
- No mention of "smart," "intelligent," "next-generation," or "cutting-edge."
- No reference to size or pricing.

## 3. Voice & Tone Guidelines

The BPxAI voice is **authoritative without being academic, specific without being technical-for-technical's-sake, confident without being boastful.**

### Five principles

1. **Name the work.** Cite NIST FIPS numbers, name the algorithms (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA), reference the protocols (TLS 1.3, X.509), point to the standards bodies. Specificity is the proof.

2. **Name the timeline.** "Harvest now, decrypt later" is a present-tense threat. The migration window for institutional cryptography is roughly 2026–2032. Say so.

3. **Name the consequence.** Don't say "improve security posture." Say "your existing TLS termination will not survive a cryptographically relevant quantum computer, and the data being recorded against it today is already exposed."

4. **Filipino-led, globally fluent.** We are a Philippine firm with international engagement experience. Our copy reflects both — referencing NIST and BSP, the FATF and the BSP-AMLC, EU AI Act and the PH Data Privacy Act.

5. **Decline to flatter.** We do not call clients "innovators," "leaders," or "visionaries." We describe what they are building and what is at risk.

### Do / don't examples

**Do:**
> NIST finalized FIPS 203, 204, and 205 in August 2024. Migration windows for institutional cryptography typically run five to seven years. Philippine financial and public-sector systems began that window twenty months ago, whether or not the migration was scoped.

**Don't:**
> The future of security is here! Get ahead of the quantum revolution with our cutting-edge solutions.

**Do:**
> Ronway scans the cryptographic surface of a target system — TLS configurations, certificate chains, signing infrastructure, key-exchange protocols — and reports the proportion of that surface that will not survive the post-quantum transition.

**Don't:**
> Ronway uses AI to make your website quantum-ready in seconds!

**Do:**
> We advise the institutions for whom failure is not a missed quarter — it is a sovereign or systemic event.

**Don't:**
> We help businesses unlock their full potential through innovative technology solutions.

### Register

- **First person:** "We" for the firm. "Our partners" for senior delivery. Never "I" except in attributed bylines.
- **Tense:** Present indicative for capabilities. Future indicative for roadmaps. Past indicative for completed engagements.
- **Sentence length:** Vary. Lead with a short declarative; follow with a longer specific. Never two long sentences in a row.
- **Numbers:** Always cite the source on first use. "NIST," "BSP Circular 1198," "FATF Recommendation 15."
- **Adjectives:** Cut by half on every pass.

## 7. The Ronway Scanner

### 7.1 What Ronway is

Ronway is BPxAI's proprietary post-quantum cryptography vulnerability scanner. Rust CLI today. Public web-based front end at `bpxai.com/ronway` on launch.

It is the first Philippine-developed PQC scanner. It is free at the point of use, public, and produces a high-level cryptographic-surface assessment for any URL submitted.

### 7.2 Why Ronway exists

Three reasons, stacked.

1. **Institutional discovery.** Most CTOs and CISOs in the Philippine market cannot today answer the question "what proportion of your cryptographic surface is quantum-vulnerable." Ronway gives them an answer in under a minute.

2. **Sovereign positioning.** A Philippine firm publishing a free, public PQC scanner — before the international consultancies have one localized for the PH market — establishes BPxAI's category leadership.

3. **The funnel.** The high-level Ronway result tells an institution *that* they have exposure. The detailed remediation plan — algorithm-by-algorithm, system-by-system — is the paid consultation. Ronway is the discovery engine for the consultancy.

### 7.3 The funnel — messaging at each stage

#### Stage one: discovery (the `/ronway` page itself)

> Most institutional systems were built on cryptographic protocols that NIST has now formally designated end-of-life for the post-quantum era. Ronway scans the public cryptographic surface of any system you nominate and returns a high-level resilience score. The scan is free. The remediation plan is the consultation.

**Hero CTA on `/ronway`:** "Scan a system." (Single URL input. No signup required for the scan itself.)

#### Stage two: high-level result (what the scan returns)

The free output is intentionally directional rather than prescriptive. It tells the institution:

- Their cryptographic resilience score (a single 0–100 number, with grade).
- The categories of exposure detected — TLS, signing, key exchange, certificate chain.
- The proportion of the scanned surface that will not survive the post-quantum transition.
- That a detailed remediation plan, algorithm-mapped and timeline-scoped, requires a consultation.

**End-of-result CTA:**
> Your resilience score is **{score}**. The detailed exposure map — which algorithms, which systems, which timelines, and how to migrate — is the next conversation. Book a Ronway consultation with our partners.

#### Stage three: the paid consultation

This is where messaging shifts from product to partner.

> A Ronway consultation is a sixty-to-ninety-minute working session with our cryptographic partners. We walk through the full exposure map of your scanned environment, identify systems Ronway could not reach from the public surface, and propose a migration sequence. The output is a written remediation brief delivered within seven days.

The pricing, packaging, and exact deliverable matrix for the paid consultation belongs in a separate document (`PRICING.md`) — not this one.

### 7.4 Tone for Ronway-specific copy

Ronway is the most public-facing surface BPxAI has. It will be linked from CISO Slack channels, regulator briefings, and the occasional viral LinkedIn post. The copy must hold up under technical scrutiny.

- Cite the standards. Always.
- Name the algorithms. Always.
- Never overclaim what the scanner does. It scans public cryptographic surface. It does not have visibility into internal HSMs, key-management systems, or air-gapped infrastructure — and the copy must say so, plainly. Honesty here is the moat.
- Where the scanner cannot reach, the consultation begins. That is the whole pitch.

## 10. Bilingual Strategy

The site continues to ship in English and Filipino. The Filipino register is the differentiator most Philippine consultancies get wrong.

### 10.1 The register problem

Most PH technology firms either ship Filipino content in conversational Taglish — heavy code-switching, casual second-person, English technical terms left untranslated — or skip Filipino entirely and ship English only.

Taglish is correct for retail, food, and consumer-banking marketing. It is wrong for an institutional consultancy.

### 10.2 The BPxAI Filipino register — three rules

**Rule 1: Formal Filipino, not Taglish.**
We use formal Filipino sentence structure and vocabulary. Where an English technical term has a working Filipino equivalent (*katibayan*, *pagsasanggalang*, *pagpapatibay*), we use the Filipino. Where there is no working equivalent (*post-quantum cryptography*, *blockchain*, *artificial intelligence*), we use the English term unchanged and let it stand inside the Filipino sentence.

**Rule 2: Plural and formal pronouns.**
We address institutions, not individuals. *Kayo*, *inyong*, *inyo*. Never *ikaw* / *iyo*. Verbs conjugate to the formal plural.

**Rule 3: Verbs of construction, not assistance.**
*Itinatayo namin* (we build). *Binubuo namin* (we construct). *Idinidisenyo namin* (we design). *Sinasanggalang namin* (we safeguard). Not *tumutulong kami* (we help) or *gumagawa kami para sa inyo* (we make for you).

### 10.3 Vocabulary shift — before and after

| English term | Old Filipino (Taglish, retail register) | New Filipino (formal, institutional register) |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Negosyo | Institusyon / Korporasyon / Organisasyon |
| Customer / client | Customer / kustomer | Kliyente |
| Build / make | Gumagawa | Itinatayo / Binubuo |
| Smart | Matalino | Mapag-alam / Mapanuri |
| Help | Tumutulong | Naglilingkod / Sumasanggalang |
| Talk to us | Kausapin kami | Makipag-ugnayan sa aming partners |
| System | Sistema | Sistema (unchanged — already correct) |
| Security | Seguridad | Pagsasanggalang / Pananggalan |
| Trust | Tiwala | Pagtitiwala / Katibayan |
| Operations | Operasyon | Operasyon (unchanged) |

### 10.4 Where Filipino leads, where English leads

- **Mission, positioning, values, vision-language** — Filipino is primary. The Filipino version is the truer version.
- **Technical specification, standards references, algorithm names** — English unchanged. *NIST FIPS 203* is *NIST FIPS 203* in both languages.
- **Calls to action** — Filipino mirrors English directly. "Brief our partners" → "Makipag-ugnayan sa aming partners." "Scan a system" → "I-scan ang isang sistema." We do not translate "Ronway." It is the product name.
- **Sector pages and POVs** — published in both languages where the audience is mixed, English-only where the audience is exclusively international.

### 10.5 What the Filipino voice signals

That BPxAI is a Filipino firm doing institutional work at international standard. That seriousness in Filipino is possible, and that we are willing to do the linguistic work other firms decline to do. For public-sector and defense engagements especially, this is not aesthetic — it is sovereign signaling.
